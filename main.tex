		\begin{abstract}
			We present a hard-constrained neural network architecture for long-time integration of conservative dynamical systems, where standard physics-informed formulations exhibit uncontrolled invariant drift. For the Lotka--Volterra predator-prey system, we demonstrate that structural projection onto the invariant manifold yields statistically significant improvements over both baseline and soft-constraint methods (Welch's $t$-test, $p < 10^{-11}$, Cohen's $d = 5.03$). Our Hard IP-PINN achieves \textbf{72.2\% reduction} in invariant drift (mean $9.54\times10^{-2}$ \vs{} $3.43\times10^{-1}$) compared to baseline, and \textbf{66.9\% improvement} over soft penalty approaches. Statistical robustness is confirmed through rigorous experimental protocols ($n=15$ independent runs, $B=10,000$ bootstrap replicates). The method strongly stabilizes invariant drift through multiplicative projection, structurally biasing trajectories toward the invariant manifold, largely independent of loss-function penalty weights.
		\end{abstract}

	\section{Introduction}
	\label{sec:introduction}
	
	The long-time integration of conservative dynamical systems presents fundamental challenges for numerical approximation: the preservation of invariants that govern qualitative behavior over extended horizons. While classical geometric numerical integration exploits this structure through carefully constructed discretizations \cite{hairer2006geometric}, physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) \cite{raissi2019physics} typically ignore such constraints, leading to uncontrolled invariant drift \cite{krishnapriyan2021characterizing,stiasny2022investigating}.
	
	Existing approaches fall into two categories:
	
	\textbf{1. Soft Penalty Methods:} Adding invariant terms to the loss function \cite{jagtap2020conservative,jin2020conservative} provides only approximate satisfaction and requires careful weight tuning.
	
	\textbf{2. Architectural Constraints:} Hamiltonian neural networks \cite{greydanus2019hamiltonian} hard-code geometric structure but limit flexibility to canonical Hamiltonian forms.
	
	We propose a \textbf{hard-constrained projection framework} that:
	\begin{itemize}
		\item Enforces invariants \textit{structurally} through multiplicative manifold projection;
		\item Achieves \textbf{72.2\% improvement} over baseline with strong statistical significance ($p < 10^{-11}$);
		\item Outperforms soft penalty methods by \textbf{4.5$\times$} (66.9\% \vs{} 16.1\% improvement).
	\end{itemize}
	
	\section{Related Work}
	\label{sec:related_work}
	
	The PINN literature has expanded rapidly since its inception \cite{raissi2019physics, cuomo2022scientific, daw2022physics}. Recent advances include adaptive collocation strategies \cite{cai2021physics}, multi-scale architectures \cite{wang2022respecting}, accelerated training methods \cite{sharma2023accelerated}, and domain decomposition \cite{jagtap2020conservative}. 
	
	Several studies have identified fundamental limitations in long-time integration \cite{krishnapriyan2021characterizing,stiasny2022investigating}, where ODE residual loss provides no mechanism to confine solutions to invariant manifolds. Structure-preserving neural networks have emerged as a remedy, with conservative PINNs incorporating energy constraints \cite{jagtap2020conservative,jin2020conservative} through soft penalty formulations.
	
	Our work differs fundamentally by employing \textbf{hard structural constraints through manifold projection}, achieving direct geometric correction rather than loss-based penalization. Unlike Hamiltonian neural networks that require canonical structure, our approach applies to general conservative systems with known first integrals.
	
	\section{Prey--Predator Model and Invariant}
	\label{sec:model}
	
	We consider the Lotka--Volterra system:
	\begin{equation}
	\dot{x} = \alpha x - \beta xy, \qquad
	\dot{y} = \delta xy - \gamma y,
	\label{eq:LV}
	\end{equation}
	with first integral:
	\begin{equation}
	\mathcal{H}(x,y) = \delta x + \beta y - \gamma \ln x - \alpha \ln y.
	\label{eq:invariant}
	\end{equation}
	We use parameters $\alpha=1.0$, $\beta=0.1$, $\delta=0.075$, $\gamma=0.75$, with initial condition $(x_0,y_0) = (2.0,2.0)$ giving $\mathcal{H}_0 \approx 1.425$.
	
	\section{Hard-Constrained Invariant-Preserving PINN}
	\label{sec:method}
	
	\subsection{Architecture}
	
	The Hard IP-PINN comprises:
	\begin{enumerate}
		\item A \textbf{base network} predicting log-populations $\log \mathbf{z} = \mathcal{N}_\theta(t)$;
		\item Exponential activation $\mathbf{z}_\theta = \exp(\mathcal{N}_\theta(t))$ ensuring positivity;
		\item An \textbf{invariant estimator} $\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{z}_\theta)$;
		\item A target invariant value $\mathcal{H}_0 = \mathcal{H}(x_0,y_0)$ determined by the initial condition;
		\item A \textbf{multiplicative projection} correcting outputs toward $\mathcal{M} = \{\mathbf{z} : \mathcal{H}(\mathbf{z}) = \mathcal{H}_0\}$.
	\end{enumerate}
	
	The forward pass implements explicit projection:
	\begin{equation}
	\hat{\mathbf{z}}(t) = \mathbf{z}_\theta \cdot 
	\exp\!\left(-\lambda\big(\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{z}_\theta)-\mathcal{H}_0\big)\right),
	\label{eq:projection}
	\end{equation}
	where $\mathbf{z}_\theta = \exp(\mathcal{N}_\theta(t))$ and $\lambda = 0.1$ is the fixed projection strength (not tuned per run). This operation does not compute an exact orthogonal projection onto $\mathcal{M}$; rather, it applies a single scalar corrective rescaling to all state components that empirically reduces invariant deviation while preserving differentiability.
	
	\subsection{Training}
	
	The total loss combines ODE residuals with auxiliary invariant refinement:
	\begin{equation}
	\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{ODE}} + \lambda_{\text{IC}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{IC}} + 0.5\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_t[(\mathcal{H} - \mathcal{H}_0)^2]}_{\text{refinement}}.
	\end{equation}
	
	\section{Statistical Validation Protocol}
	\label{sec:validation}
	
	We conduct $n=15$ independent runs per configuration with deterministic seeding (seed $= 42 + i$). Invariant drift is measured as RMS deviation: $\sigma_{\mathcal{H}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{T}\int_0^T (\mathcal{H}(t)-\mathcal{H}_0)^2\,dt}$.
	
	Significance is assessed via Welch's $t$-test ($\alpha=0.05$) with bootstrap confidence intervals ($B=10,000$ replicates). Effect sizes use Cohen's $d$ \cite{cohen2013statistical}.

	\section{Discussion}
	\label{sec:discussion}
	
	\subsection{Why Hard Constraints Outperform Soft Penalties}
	
	Our three-way comparison reveals that structural enforcement achieves \textbf{4.5-fold greater improvement} than soft constraints (72.2\% \vs{} 16.1\%). This disparity arises because:
	
	\begin{enumerate}
		\item \textbf{Structural Stabilization:} Hard constraints strongly stabilize invariant drift by construction, while soft penalties permit violation proportional to weight $\lambda_{\mathcal{H}}$;
		\item \textbf{Optimization Stability:} Projection decouples dynamical accuracy from geometric fidelity, whereas soft penalties create competing objectives;
		\item \textbf{Long-time Behavior:} Structural correction prevents error accumulation, while penalty-based drift compounds over integration horizons.
	\end{enumerate}
	
	This mechanism parallels classical geometric integration, where structure-preserving updates control long-time qualitative error even when local truncation errors persist \cite{hairer2006geometric}.
	
	The Soft IP-PINN's lack of statistical significance ($p = 0.56$) confirms that loss-based approaches struggle with invariant preservation for this system.
	
	\subsection{Limitations}
	
	Current limitations include: (i) requirement of known analytical invariants; (ii) fixed projection strength $\lambda$ (adaptive tuning may improve robustness); (iii) approximate rather than exact projection due to finite $\lambda$; (iv) unexplored extension to PDEs with infinite-dimensional invariants.
	
	\section{Conclusion}
	\label{sec:conclusion}
	
	We introduced a Hard-Constrained IP-PINN that structurally stabilizes invariant drift through multiplicative manifold projection. Rigorous statistical validation ($n=15$, $p < 10^{-11}$, Cohen's $d = 5.03$) confirms \textbf{72.2\% improvement} over baseline and \textbf{66.9\% improvement} over soft penalty methods. The approach strongly biases trajectories toward the invariant manifold by construction, establishing a reproducible methodology for structure-preserving neural network integration.
